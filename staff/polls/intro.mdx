# Polls Overview

Polls are for quick, live questions during class. You write one question with a fixed list of choices, open the poll, project the QR code, and watch a bar chart fill in as students answer. Polls are not graded and are not tied to assignments.

## Key Features

- **Fast creation**: Write the question in the visual builder, or paste a JSON payload
- **Two question types**: Multiple Choice and Single Choice, both offering a fixed list of choices
- **Live bar chart**: One horizontal bar chart of responses per choice, updating as answers arrive
- **Presentation mode**: A fullscreen view of that chart for classroom projection, with the QR code in the corner
- **Manual closing**: A poll stays open until you close it
- **Anonymous or signed in**: Choose whether students must log in to answer

<Note>
Polls are a course feature flag. When **Polls** is turned off under [**Course Settings → Feature flags**](/staff/feature-flags), the **Polls** menu entry disappears for students and staff alike, though direct links to poll pages still work.
</Note>

## Use Cases

Polls work well for:

- **Comprehension checks**: See whether the room follows a concept before you move on
- **Discussion starters**: Gather opinions and use the split to open a discussion
- **Feedback**: Ask about pace or clarity mid-lecture
- **Concept voting**: Let students choose which topic to cover next

<Warning>
Polls are not an attendance tool. Even with **Require login to respond** turned on, the results page shows only the count per choice. There is no roster, no respondent list, and no export, so you cannot tell from the interface who answered.
</Warning>

## One poll link per course

Every poll in a course shares one link, `https://<your-pawtograder-host>/poll/<course_id>`, and that link always serves the poll that was opened most recently. The QR code encodes the same link.

Open one poll at a time. If two polls are live at once, students who scan the code reach the newer one, and the older poll has no link of its own to hand out. This is a known issue with an open report against Pawtograder, and a fix is on the way.

## Polls vs Surveys

| Feature | Polls | Surveys |
|---------|-------|---------|
| Purpose | Quick in-class questions | Detailed feedback collection |
| Duration | Open until you close it | Configurable due dates |
| Question count | One question per poll | Multiple questions |
| Repeat responses | One per student when login is required, unlimited when it is not | One response per student. Editable before submitting, and after submitting only if [response editing](/staff/surveys#response-editing-control) is on |
| Results | Live bar chart of counts per choice | Per-response table, analytics, and CSV export |
| Best for | Lectures, quick checks | Course evaluations, detailed feedback |

## Getting Started

Choose **Polls** from your course navigation to reach **Manage Polls**, the list of every poll in the course. From there you create polls, open and close them, and open a poll's results page.

<Note>
Creating, opening, closing, and deleting polls is limited to instructors and graders in that course. Graders have the same poll permissions as instructors.
</Note>

Running your first poll takes eight steps:

1. Choose **Polls** in your course navigation, then click **+ Create Poll**
2. Click **Open Visual Builder**, enter your question prompt, pick **Multiple Choice** or **Single Choice**, and add your choices
3. Click **Use This Poll** to write the question back into the **Poll Question JSON** field
4. Optionally click **Preview Poll** to see the student view
5. Decide whether to check **Require login to respond**
6. Click **Publish Poll**, which opens the poll immediately, or **Save as Draft** to open it later
7. Open the poll's results page, project the **Answer at:** link and QR code, and click **Present** for fullscreen
8. Click **Stop Poll** when you are done collecting responses

See [Creating Polls](/staff/polls/creating-polls) for the full flow and [Poll Visualizations](/staff/polls/visualizations) for what the results page shows.
