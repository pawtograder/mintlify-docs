# Audit Log

**Course Settings → Audit Log** is a read-only table of row-level changes to your
course's data: who changed what, when, and from which IP address. The menu entry
is instructor-only, and the page lives at
`/course/<course_id>/manage/course/audit`.

There is nothing to configure. The log is written by database triggers that are
always on; you cannot add tables to it, turn it off for your course, or edit or
delete an entry once it exists.

<Warning>
**The page currently shows an empty table, even when the log is full.** Verified
against a running course: the table renders with all its headers, reports
`0 Rows` and `Page 1 of 0`, and shows no error — while the underlying table held
over five thousand rows for that same course.

The cause is a missing database policy. Row-level security is enabled on the
audit table with no policy attached, and in that state Postgres returns zero
rows rather than refusing the request, so the app receives an empty result and
renders it faithfully. The policy that admits instructors exists on the older
audit table and was not carried over when the log was repartitioned.

The important consequence is that an empty audit log is **not** evidence that
nothing happened in your course. Until the policy is restored, reading the log
requires database access — ask a Pawtograder administrator.
</Warning>

## What gets recorded

Every recorded change produces one row with these fields:

- `created_at` — when the change happened.
- `user_id` — the account that made the change, resolved against your roster for
  display.
- `table` — the database table that changed.
- `old` and `new` — the full row before and after, as JSON.
- `ip_addr` — the first address in the request's `x-forwarded-for` header.

The page's own description lists ten resources: assignments, assignment due date
exceptions, assignment groups, discussion threads, rubric checks, rubric
criteria, rubric parts, submissions, submission reviews, and submission comments
(both global comments and line annotations).

That list is incomplete. Triggers also record changes to gradebook columns,
gradebook cell values, profiles, student tags, lab sections, lab section
meetings, and assignment group membership. So the log covers more than the page
advertises.

## What does not get recorded

- **Most deletions.** Insert and update are recorded everywhere, but delete is
  recorded only for rubric parts, criteria and checks, due date exceptions,
  assignment group membership, lab section meetings, tags, gradebook columns,
  and gradebook cell values. Deleting an assignment, a submission, a submission
  comment, a discussion thread, an assignment group, a profile, or a lab section
  leaves no audit row.
- **Reads.** The log records writes. Nobody's viewing of a submission, a grade,
  or a student's profile is recorded.
- **Anything outside those tables.** Office hours activity, help requests,
  polls, surveys, flashcards, enrollment changes, feature flag changes, emails
  sent from the **Emailer**, and workflow runs are not in the audit log.

## Can you see who changed a grade?

For grades, yes — with a caveat about which column tells you who.

- **Gradebook cell edits and overrides** are recorded, as inserts, updates, and
  deletes on the gradebook's per-student values.
- **Gradebook column changes** — adding a column, editing its expression or
  maximum, releasing it — are recorded.
- **Rubric-based grading** is recorded through submission reviews and through
  submission comments and line annotations, which is where applied rubric checks
  live.

<Warning>
The column labeled **Student Name** is not the student the row is about. It is
the name of the account that performed the change, looked up in your roster. To
find who edited a particular student's grade, filter on the **Table** and
**Resource ID** columns and read **Student Name** as "actor".

When a change came from a background job rather than a signed-in person — the
autograder writing a submission, a gradebook recalculation — there is no signed-in
user to record, and the column is blank. In practice that is most rows: every one
of the five thousand rows inspected on a live course had no recorded actor, so
this column would render blank throughout even once the log becomes readable.
</Warning>

## Reading the table

The table has six columns: **Date**, **Student Name**, **IP Address**,
**Table**, **Resource ID**, and **Change**. Rows are sorted newest-first by
default; click any header to sort by it.

**Change** shows only the top-level fields whose values differ, as `Was:` and
`Now:` pairs. A field that gained a value shows just the new value; a field that
lost one shows `Removed`. Hover a field name for the raw JSON of both sides.
Fields whose values are themselves objects or arrays are left out of the diff,
and if neither `old` nor `new` is an object the cell reads **Change not
displayable as object diff**.

**Resource ID** is read from the `id` of the *new* row, so it is empty for
recorded deletions — a Resource ID filter will never match one.

### Filtering

Every column header has a text box under it. Typing in it filters on a
case-insensitive substring match, in your browser, against the rows already
loaded — not a database query. The **Date** box matches the date as it is
rendered, so you are searching the displayed text rather than a date range;
there is no from/to date picker.

<Note>
The page loads every audit row for your course before filtering or paginating
any of them. On a busy course this makes the first load slow. Paging controls at
the bottom let you show 25, 50 (the default), 100, 200, or 500 rows at a time,
and the row count under them reflects your active filters.
</Note>

### There is no export

The audit log has no download or **Export to CSV** control, unlike most other
staff tables. See [Exporting Course Data](/staff/exports) for what can be
exported. If you need audit rows in a file, ask a Pawtograder administrator.

## How far back the log goes

**90 days.** The audit table is partitioned by day, and a maintenance job drops
every partition older than 90 days. It runs nightly, and dropped days are gone
permanently — there is no archive or cold copy inside the product.

The 90-day window is fixed in the database, not a course or deployment setting.

<Warning>
If you need a change history to survive longer than 90 days — for an academic
integrity case, a grade appeal, or an institutional records requirement — you
have to arrange it before the ninetieth day, and you cannot do it from this
page. Ask a Pawtograder administrator: an operator can preserve a specific day's
partition instead of letting it drop.

The audit log is the only part of your course's data that prunes itself. Its
90-day window is much shorter than the retention period covering everything
else, so do not treat the audit log as the long-term record of your course.
</Warning>

## Who can read it

The **Audit Log** menu entry appears for instructors only; graders do not see
it. The route guard on staff pages admits both instructors and graders, so a
grader who types the URL reaches the page — but the log's data is not exposed to
either role at the database level, which is the access limit that actually
applies.
